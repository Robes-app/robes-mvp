# Stylist reference — how a professional packs

**Source:** Editorialist Private Client mailer, *"The packing list your closet has been waiting for"* —
Stefania Allen, Co-Founder & Creative Director. Received 29 Jul 2026.
**Why it's in the repo:** external corroboration for the Look-as-entity brief. A working
stylist's method, in her own words, is the same object model we're building — kept here so
the copy and the mechanics can be checked against it rather than against our own assumptions.

---

## The thesis, in her words

> "Whenever I'm packing for a trip, I don't start with a suitcase — I start with outfits."

> "The biggest mistake most people make when packing? They pack individual pieces instead of
> complete looks. A dress here, a pair of shoes there — and suddenly you arrive somewhere
> beautiful with a suitcase full of clothes and nothing to wear."

> "Before anything goes into the bag, I think in outfits. My rule: if I can't think of at least
> three ways to wear something, it stays home."

## What it corroborates

| Her method | Our object model |
|---|---|
| Thinks in outfits before pieces | `Look` is the unit; the wardrobe of pieces is the vocabulary |
| "Three looks. Every trip." — daytime / evening / versatile | Looks are countable, reusable objects, not positions in a plan blob |
| "Swap the accessories, change the shoe — same outfit, entirely different energy" | Small edits keep a Look's identity (brief §2). This is exactly why swap must not mint a new Look |
| "If I can't think of three ways to wear it, it stays home" | Wear counts and cost-per-wear are the measurable form of the same rule |
| Curates looks per part of the trip, then packs | Plans-first, pin later — a look is styled once and pinned to the days it serves |

## What it tells us about register

The mailer never explains its own mechanics. It states the method once, shows three looks, and
stops. No progress bars, no scores, no instruction copy telling the reader what to do next —
which is the same restraint the brief asks for in A7 (*tactile, not gamified*) and the reason
the wireframes' explanatory lines were not carried into the build.

Her sentences are short, declarative, first-person, and about clothes — never about the tool.
"Same outfit, entirely different energy" is the tone to aim for. "Robes keeps a labelled
positive example of what you reach for" is not.
