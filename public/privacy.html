Privacy notice

Plain English. Last updated 13 November 2026.

Who we are

Robes is run by Robes Technologies Limited, 39 Seapark Road, Clontarf, Dublin D03 R673, Ireland. We decide how your data is used, which makes us its controller under European data protection law. Anything about it: hello@byrobes.com.

What we collect

Your email, name and the styling inputs you give us — prompts, wardrobe pieces, any photos you upload (including the optional close-up under Your digital twin), and the facts you give about your size. We also record how you use Robes: the pages you open and the actions you take. Robes is for adults. You must be 18 or over, and we do not knowingly hold data about anyone younger.

Your location

If you allow it, Robes reads your location from your device to dress you for the weather, and for nothing else. Your device sends your position to Open-Meteo, a European weather service, to name your town and fetch the forecast. Your town and the forecast, never your coordinates, reach our servers and go into the brief for your looks. Your location is not stored. Decline and Robes styles you without the forecast.

How it’s used

Only to run Robes for you: creating looks and analyses, storing your wardrobe and style profile, and making Robes better. We never sell your data and never use it for advertising.

Where it goes

These services process your data for us:

  • Your account, wardrobe, looks and style profile live in a database hosted by Supabase in Frankfurt, Germany.
  • Your photographs, your model and the pictures of your looks are stored on Cloudinary, in the United States.
  • Your photos and prompts are sent to Google Gemini solely to produce your results. Google may process them outside the EU, including in the United States, and does not use them to improve its models.
  • Emails are sent through Resend, in the United States. Waitlist and feedback records live in Airtable, in the United States.
  • How the app is used is measured in PostHog, on European servers.
  • Our own servers run on Railway.

Where a service is outside the EU, the transfer is covered by the European Commission’s Standard Contractual Clauses in our agreement with that service.

Your digital twin

If you add a close-up under Your digital twin, Robes uses it twice. It reads your colouring from it, and it uses it as the reference for your model — a full-length image of you, created from your face in the photograph and from the facts you give (height, size, bust, waist, age). Every look Robes then composes for you is rendered on that model, so the pictures of your looks carry your face. The model image is created by Google Gemini from your close-up and your facts, and it is stored on Cloudinary alongside your wardrobe photographs. Nothing is read from your body in a photograph: your line comes from the facts and the sliders you set, and you can change either at any time. Remove the close-up and the model is redrawn without your face; delete your account and the model goes with it. Your looks are seen by no one else unless you share one: a shared look becomes a page anyone with its link can open.

Session recordings

So we can see where Robes is confusing, PostHog records sessions in the app — the pages you open, what you click, and what you type, including the prompts you write. Your wardrobe photographs appear in them, because they are on the screen as you work. Passwords are never recorded. Recordings are kept on European servers for the period our PostHog plan sets, are visible only to us, and are never used for advertising. Ask and we will stop recording yours.

Emails from Robes

Robes can email you: when the three looks you asked for are ready, the odd note when your wardrobe is ready for more (a look waiting on your model, pieces Robes is borrowing, a week with nothing planned), and, if you switch it on, a morning line on the days you have planned. Every one of these is a choice you make in the app — the looks-ready note and the nudges with one tap on your first day, the morning line under Account — and every email carries a one-click link to stop it. There are no tracking pixels: we record that an email was sent, and whether you opened Robes from its link, nothing more.

Receipts you forward

If you forward an order confirmation to your own Robes address (yours alone, shown under Add a piece → Forward a receipt), Robes reads the email to find the pieces in it — their names, brands, colours, prices, sizes and product photographs — and holds them on your wardrobe page until you look them over. When the receipt has been read, Robes emails you to say so, with a link to the review; that email carries the same one-click stop as every other. Nothing is filed into your wardrobe without you. The reading is done by Google Gemini, the same model that reads your photographs; the email’s text is kept only as the pieces it produced, and product images are copied to Cloudinary so they survive the retailer’s link expiring. Forward only emails you are happy for Robes to read.

How long we keep it

  • Your account, wardrobe, looks, style profile, close-up and model: for as long as your account exists. Delete your account and they are deleted at once; copies in backups and on our image host clear within 30 days.
  • Your location: never stored.
  • Session recordings and usage counts: for the period our PostHog plan sets. The record of emails sent: for as long as your account exists.
  • Waitlist and feedback records: until you ask us to remove them, or two years after we last hear from you.
  • Server logs: up to 30 days.

The legal basis

  • Our contract with you covers running Robes: your account, wardrobe, looks, style profile and the emails that are part of the service.
  • Your consent covers your close-up and the model drawn from it, your location, the nudges and the morning line. You give it by adding the photo, allowing location or switching the emails on, and withdraw it by removing or switching them off.
  • Our legitimate interest in keeping Robes working, secure and clear covers usage counts, session recordings and server logs.

Your rights

You can see, correct, download or delete what Robes holds about you, object to how it is used, or withdraw consent — in the app or by email, and we answer within a month. You can also complain to the Data Protection Commission, Ireland’s regulator.

Your choices

The close-up is optional — every step that asks for a photograph can be skipped, and a model can be drawn from your facts alone. You can update or remove wardrobe pieces at any time. Delete your account under Settings › Account › Delete account: it removes your wardrobe, looks, style profile and model. Email us if you would rather we did it, or to have your session recordings deleted.

Contact
